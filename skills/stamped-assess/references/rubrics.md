# Rubrics

Record a use case with objective, audience, and description.
A rubric addresses all seven principles with priority, direction, and rationale.
Priorities are essential, important, supporting, or out of scope.
Directions are maximize, balance, or preserve; the explanation names the relevant component and desired property.
These choices do not change the normative requirement levels in the pinned STAMPED sources.

Use a small number of observable criteria initially.
Each names its category, question, target, rationale, and required evidence activities.
Map relevant checklist items by their exact IDs; add criteria beyond the checklist when the objective requires them.
An unmapped criterion is valid and should explain its purpose.
Do not claim selected criteria exhaustively cover an entire principle.

Activities include inspect, resolve, retrieve, construct, execute, compare, and independent_reproduce.
They are capabilities, not a fixed effort ranking.
A stronger claim may require different activities or a different access context.
If the agreed scope changes, retain the reason and issue a new rubric or assessment record where necessary.

For journalism, distinguish preserving a historical source capture from rerunning a changing live acquisition.
Make denominators, inclusion rules, and the evidence behind published claims inspectable.
For manuscript or presentation preservation, durable sources and assets can coexist with a disposable build environment.
For controlled research data, state the intended recipient and authorized access procedure without disclosing sensitive material.
These are examples of criteria design, not universal required toolchains.
